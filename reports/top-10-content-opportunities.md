Builds on `reports/technical-html-priority.md` (the original 20-item ranking from the investigation phase) — this report re-derives the ordering against the newly specified 6-factor framework rather than just reusing the old list, and adds the AI-citation dimension that wasn't scored before. **No pages built or edited.**

## Why these and not generic blogs

Per your instruction to avoid generic informational blogs unless there's a clear strategic reason: every entry below is either (a) a specific product page with a proven or category-justified purchase decision behind it, or (b) in one case, a genuine strategic exception — closing a content gap where a blog post already has real, measured search demand but no corresponding product-level technical page to convert that demand. No purely informational topic ("what is a refractory," "history of refractories," etc.) made this list.

## Scoring notes

- **GSC demand**: from the live 30-day pull (100-row free-tier cap — most individual product pages don't surface here at all; "no row" is stated explicitly, not treated as zero)
- **Commercial intent**: is this a recurring, technically-differentiated plant-consumable (high) or a lower-frequency/lower-differentiation purchase (medium)
- **Product importance**: does it complete a family where sibling products already have datasheets and traction
- **Technical data availability**: does `productCatalogData.js` already have enough raw spec data (grades, chemistry, specs) to write a real datasheet without inventing figures, or would it require new research
- **International opportunity**: does this product map to an industry already named as an export target (GCC/ASEAN steel, cement, glass, aluminium)
- **AI citation potential**: does the product have a crisp, quotable technical distinction (a specific number, a clear comparison, a named test standard) that an AI answer engine could lift as a direct citation — vague or purely descriptive content scores lower here regardless of traffic
